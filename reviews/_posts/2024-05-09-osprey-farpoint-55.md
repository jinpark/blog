> **TL;DR**
> 
> Buy it if you want to carry more than a standard backpack but you don't want to lug around an extra carryon luggage. 
> For most people, I think having a decent sized normal backpack with a seperate daypack/totebag is good enough.

---

Osprey Farpoint 55 is one of the most recommended backpacks for travel backpacks and for good reason. The whole bag is well designed to be able to carry a lot of gear in a comfortable way. There are a few interesting features about this bag which is uncommon compared to other backpacks that you might have used.

There is a newer version of the bag with slight ergonomic improvements but I think my review should work for the newer bag as well.

Pros:

1. Removable daypack

This is the most interesting part of the bag. The backpack is actually two backpacks, one large duffel like backpack and one small daybag. The main bag is a large open compartment with internal compression straps. 

The daypack is a small backpack with a laptop compartment. It also has 2 exterior bottle holders. It is the perfect size for a day pack and should allow you to keep small necessities with you.

2. Multiple Carry Methods

Main bag: There are padded handles on the long side of the bag to allow you to carry the bag in a briefcase style. The backpack straps can be hidden and zipped into the bag to keep the straps from dragging on the ground. There is a hipbelt to help you carry the bag easier.

The two bags are normally zipped together with the daypack zipped on top of the main bag, but this can be changed to allow you to have the daypack on the front and the main bag on the back. This allows you to have better weight distribution.


3. Carry on size
   
The main bag when separated from the daypack is technically not carry on size but I have never had any trouble with it as a carry on in a full service airline. I feel that would not be the case with a low cost carrier or budget airline like ryan air or easyjet. I did use it once in korea with jin air and had no issues. so ymmv.

Cons:

1. No organization in the main compartment

Using packing cubes is a must for the main compartment. Since there is no internal organization in the bag, you have to use your own packing cubes and bags to keep things organized and not flying around. This might be a pro for you if you need to bring something big and bulky.

2. Weight Distribution

The weight distribution of the whole bag when the daypack has a laptop and a charger inside is very poor. Since the heaviest part of the bag is furthest away from your body, the whole bag sags away from you making it feel heavier and less stable. Osprey did think of this and allows you to clip the daypack to the bag so it is in front of you, but when it is in the standard position, it feels off.

3. Limited usefulness

This might just be my opinion but theres a sweet spot for this bag. It is most useful when you want to carry everything on your back without having a carry-on carrier bag. Mostly when you will be doing a lot of walking or the roads or streets you will be walking on is not paved or is all cobblestone making it difficult to roll around. If that is not the case, a carry on and a bigger backpack with a folded totebag is a better choice.


Overall, I really like this bag for longer trips where I dont want to carry around a carrier. Having a large backpack is sometimes very useful but not always.
